# Statement Analysis: What Do Suspects' Words Really Reveal?

**Author:** Susan H. Adams, M.A., Special Agent, FBI Academy  
**Publication:** *FBI Law Enforcement Bulletin*, October 1996, Volume 65, Number 10, pp. 12–20  
**Publisher:** Federal Bureau of Investigation, U.S. Department of Justice (NCJRS Document No. 165247)  
**Classification:** Forensic Linguistics / Investigative Statement Analysis  

---

## 1. Abstract & Executive Overview

Statement analysis is an investigative methodology that scrutinizes the linguistic composition of written and spoken accounts independently of known case facts. Rooted in psycholinguistics, discourse analysis, and behavioral analysis, the discipline operates on the premise that when individuals fabricate narratives or conceal truth, cognitive and psychological stress alters their lexical and grammatical choices.

Truthful statements reflect direct cognitive recall of episodic memory, characterized by natural linguistic ownership, chronological congruence, and contextual equilibrium. Deceptive statements, conversely, manifest unconscious psychological distancing, defensive syntactic hedging, narrative disproportion, and subtle avoidance of definitive commitment.

Statement analysis serves as a force-multiplier for investigators, corporate auditors, and forensic analysts by identifying:
1. Areas of omission and concealment.
2. Unconscious linguistic markers of guilt or deceit.
3. Critical deception points to be interrogated in formal cross-examination.

---

## 2. The Methodological Protocol: The Two-Step Analysis

Statement analysis proceeds through a strict two-stage analytical model:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ STAGE 1: ESTABLISH THE LINGUISTIC NORM (BASELINE)      │
│ Determine standard syntactic, grammatical, and         │
│ cognitive patterns expected in truthful recollection.  │
└──────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────┘
                           │
                           ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ STAGE 2: DETECT & QUANTIFY DEVIATIONS                  │
│ Identify lexical shifts, pronoun dropping, temporal    │
│ anomalies, and structural disproportion.               │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Stage 1: Establishing the Norm
A truthful recounting of an event exhibits consistent baseline characteristics:
- **Consistent Voice:** Sustained first-person singular perspective (*I, me, my*).
- **Temporal Congruence:** Narrative delivered in simple past tense (*went, saw, demanded*).
- **Proportional Structure:** Normal chronological distribution across pre-incident, incident, and post-incident phases.
- **Direct Commitment:** Unambiguous verbs and absolute assertions without unsolicited qualifiers.

### Stage 2: Identifying Deviations
Departures from the baseline signal psychological tension. Whenever a subject alters parts of speech, transitions across verb tenses, drops personal pronouns, or introduces extraneous narrative justification, the analyst flags the passage as a **critical point of deception or psychological conflict**.

## 3. Four Core Diagnostic Pillars

### Pillar I: Parts of Speech (Pronouns, Nouns, Verbs)

#### 1. Personal & Possessive Pronouns
Pronouns reflect the psychological ownership and relational closeness between the speaker, their actions, and other individuals.

* **First-Person Singular (*I, me, my*):**
  * *Truthful Baseline:* Direct ownership of behavior (*"I parked the vehicle, locked the door, and entered the facility"*).
  * *Deceptive Evasion (Pronoun Dropping):* Deleting the subject pronoun to avoid personal attachment (*"Parked the vehicle, locked the door, entered the facility"*). The omission is an unconscious attempt to avoid explicitly stating that the speaker performed the act.
* **First-Person Plural (*We, us, our*):**
  * *Psychological Shift:* Sudden transitions from *"I"* to *"we"* indicate an attempt to diffuse solitary responsibility or dilute guilt among a collective.
  * *Premature Plurality:* Introducing *"we"* before an encounter has logically formed signals fabrication.
* **Possessive Pronoun Alteration (Psychological Distancing):**
  * A change from possessive indicators (*"my husband", "my car", "our office"*) to definite articles or demonstrative adjectives (*"the husband", "the car", "that building", "this woman"*) signals emotional detachment, hostility, or psychological preparation for loss/alienation.
  * *Forensic Example (Susan Smith Case):* Referring to missing children as *"the children"* or *"my kids would never..."* (using past/conditional tense before any confirmation of death).

#### 2. Verb Tenses & Temporal Congruence
* **The Rule of Past Tense:** Recounting historical memory demands the past tense (*"I drove", "He entered", "They signed"*).
* **The Present Tense Leakage:** Shifting into present tense (*"I drive down the street, and this guy comes out..."*) indicates that the subject is not retrieving an episodic memory from past storage; rather, they are actively visualizing and constructing the scene in real time.
* **Passive Voice Transmutation:** Replacing active voice (*"I broke the vase"*) with passive voice (*"The vase got broken"*) removes the causative agent from the act.

---

### Pillar II: Extraneous Information & Narrative Padding

Truthful individuals focus on the substantive facts of what transpired. Deceptive subjects, anxious that their narrative appears insufficient or seeking to project a fabricated persona of virtue, saturate the statement with irrelevant details.

* **Extraneous Filler:** Extensive exposition regarding mundane routines (e.g., precise menus, weather commentary, detailed routine decisions) that precede or follow the critical event, contrasted against zero detail regarding the event itself.
* **Protestations of Character / Unsolicited Oaths:**
  * Inserting religious or moral assertions (*"I am an honest Christian man", "I would swear on my mother's grave", "As God is my witness"*).
  * In forensic linguistics, unsolicited pledges of integrity correlate inversely with veracity. Honest subjects assume their credibility; deceptive subjects labor to manufacture it.

---

### Pillar III: Lack of Conviction & Equivocation

Deceptive narrators avoid making unequivocal statements because direct lies incur heavy psychological resistance. Consequently, they deploy linguistic softeners, qualifiers, and evasive formulations.

* **Hedging Modifiers:** Words that degrade absolute certainty (*"kind of", "sort of", "basically", "mostly", "I believe", "to the best of my recollection", "if I recall correctly"*).
* **Defective Denials:**
  * *Truthful Denial Formula:* *"I did not do X"* (Subject + Absolute Negative + Exact Accused Action).
  * *Deceptive / Equivocal Substitutes:*
    * *"I would never do that"* (Speculation on moral disposition rather than factual refutation).
    * *"Why would I do something like that?"* (Answering an accusation with an analytical evasion).
    * *"I could not have done that"* (Shifting from factual action to capability/feasibility).

---

### Pillar IV: Statement Balance & Structural Geometry

A narrative is a chronological construct. In genuine trauma or real episodic recall, the cognitive distribution of memory adheres to an empirical proportion:

```
TRUTHFUL STATEMENT BALANCE:
[ 20% - 25% ]  Prologue / Setup (leading up to incident)
[ 50% - 60% ]  The Critical Event (focal interaction, central conflict)
[ 20% - 25% ]  Epilogue / Aftermath (consequences, actions taken after)
```

#### Diagnostic Pathologies in Statement Balance:
1. **The Truncated Incident (The Compression Hole):**
   * The narrator spends 80% of the statement detailing the morning routine, traffic, and conversations before the incident.
   * The actual incident is collapsed into one hurried sentence (*"Then the incident happened and things went crazy"*).
   * The aftermath is entirely ignored or omitted.
   * **Diagnostic Implication:** Fabrication or acute guilt. The subject dreads inventing specific sensory details about the critical event because false details provide investigators with objective points to disprove.
2. **Missing Post-Incident Reality:**
   * Genuine victims detail the emotional, logistical, and medical shock following an assault or loss.
   * Deceptive subjects stop immediately at the climax, failing to provide post-event logic, police notification steps, or recovery actions.

## 4. Analytical Scoring Matrix for Investigative Audits

| Linguistic Feature | Truthful Indicator | Deceptive / Evasive Indicator | Diagnostic Red Flag |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Personal Pronouns** | Consistent *"I", "me", "my"* across actions | Pronoun deletion (*"Left building"*) or shift to *"we"* | High risk of responsibility evasion |
| **Possessive Reference** | *"My car", "my partner", "our funds"* | *"The vehicle", "that person", "the asset"* | Psychological detachment / alienative posture |
| **Verb Tense** | Consistent simple past tense | Sudden switch to present tense (*"he says"*) | Active cognitive fabrication |
| **Statement Balance** | ~25% Intro / ~50% Event / ~25% Aftermath | 80% Intro / 5% Event / 15% Aftermath | Critical event suppression |
| **Denial Architecture** | Direct: *"I did not steal the files"* | Softened: *"I wouldn't ever do that"* | Equivocal avoidance of factual denial |
| **Spontaneous Detail** | Contextual, sensory, imperfect recall | Highly structured, sterile, or padded with oaths | Rehearsed script or over-justification |
