## 1. Executive Summary & The Memory Source Monitoring Paradigm

The **Reality Monitoring (RM)** framework originates from cognitive memory research by Marcia Johnson and Carol Raye. It explains how the human brain differentiates between:
1. **Memories derived from external perception** (events that actually happened in the physical world).
2. **Memories derived from internal processes** (imaginations, dreams, fantasies, thoughts, or fabricated lies).

When applied to forensic deception detection by Siegfried Sporer, the central hypothesis asserts:
> **"Statements based on real perceptual experiences contain fundamentally different qualitative memory attributes than statements generated through imagination, rehearsal, or intentional fabrication."**

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE REALITY MONITORING DICHOTOMY                     │
├───────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────┤
│ EXTERNAL PERCEPTION (REALITY)     │ INTERNAL IMAGINATION (FABRICATION) │
├───────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • High Sensory Details (visual,   │ • Dominated by Cognitive Operations│
│   auditory, olfactory, tactile)   │   ("I reasoned", "I concluded")    │
│ • Concrete Spatial Dimensions     │ • High Generalizations & Schemas   │
│ • Granular Temporal Placement     │ • Abstract, Logical Justifications │
│ • Idiosyncratic Emotional Fluency │ • Low Sensory Anchor Points        │
└───────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 2. Core Reality Monitoring Criteria in Forensic Text Analysis

Sporer and Masip established 8 core diagnostic criteria used to assess verbal and written statements:

### Category I: Perceptual Attributes (Sensory Imprints)
1. **Visual Details:** Mentions of colors, lighting, physical dimensions, movements, clothing, or physical postures (*"The red reflection on the damp hood of the car"*).
2. **Auditory Details:** Direct sound recollections, tone of voice, environmental noises, accents, or background auditory cues (*"The metallic click of the gate latch"*, *"He spoke with a rasping drawl"*).
3. **Olfactory, Gustatory & Tactile Sensations:** Smells, tastes, textures, temperature, or physical sensations (*"The burning smell of old brake pads"*, *"The freezing rain soaked through my gloves"*).

### Category II: Contextual Dimensions (Spatiotemporal Anchoring)
4. **Spatial Details:** Precise spatial relationships, layout of objects, distances, and relative positioning of actors (*"He was standing three feet to the left of the counter, between the register and the emergency door"*).
5. **Temporal Details:** Specific time sequences, progression of daylight, chronological landmarks, and duration perceptions (*"It took roughly two minutes for the engine to cool"*, *"The dusk was settling as the sirens started"*).

### Category III: Affective and Cognitive States
6. **Affective Information:** Explicit descriptions of physiological feelings and emotional sensations experienced at the exact moment of the event (*"My heart pounded against my ribs and my hands went completely numb"*).
7. **Cognitive Operations (The Negative / Deceptive Marker):**  
   * **Definition:** Linguistic formulations that describe inferences, deductions, or rationalizations rather than perceptual memory (*"I must have left the door unlocked because the handle moved easily"*, *"I thought to myself that this looked suspicious"*, *"I deduced that..."*).
   * **Diagnostic Principle:** High levels of cognitive operations relative to perceptual details indicate that the narrator is **reconstructing or fabricating** the scene through logic rather than retrieving a stored perceptual trace.
8. **Realism / Plausibility:** Whether the account respects the physical, psychological, and logistical realities of human behavior without theatrical hyperbole.

---

## 3. Mathematical Formula: The Reality Monitoring Ratio (RMR)

In forensic linguistics, the ratio of sensory-contextual details to cognitive operations serves as a quantitative index of veracity:

$$\text{RMR} = \frac{\text{Perceptual Details} + \text{Spatial Details} + \text{Temporal Details}}{\text{Cognitive Operations} + 1}$$

* **Truthful Experience Profile ($\text{RMR} \ge 2.5$):** Rich in raw sensory and spatial anchors; low in speculative reasoning.
* **Fabricated / Scripted Profile ($\text{RMR} \le 1.0$):** High in internal reasoning and justifications; impoverished in sensory and spatial detail.

---

## 4. Key Takeaways for Subtext Engine

1. **Sensory Density Screening:** Truthful accounts describe *what was seen, heard, and physically felt*. Fabrications describe *what was thought, concluded, or intended*.
2. **Cognitive Operation Warning:** Excessive deployment of words like *"I assumed"*, *"I figured"*, *"It seemed logical"*, or *"My impression was"* signals a narrator filling gaps in memory or inventing an alibi through cognitive deduction.
