## 1. Executive Summary: The Structural Geometry of Spontaneous Memory

Investigative Discourse Analysis evaluates how an individual organizes language when describing a past event under conditions of high cognitive and emotional stakes. 

The core forensic premise states:
> **"Genuine experiential memory produces an unbroken, associative, chronologically anchored narrative. When an individual attempts to deceive, omit critical actions, or alter reality, the linguistic fabric breaks down through syntactic stalling, terminology shifts, and unprompted cognitive padding."**

## 2. Empirically Validated Discourse Indicators

### 2.1 The Noun Inversion & Terminology Shift Axiom
In truthful recall, an individual maintains a consistent mental representation of significant people and objects.
* **The Diagnostic Phenomenon:** When a writer shifts their terminology for a constant entity across the timeline:
  * Example: Beginning with *"my colleague"*, switching to *"that individual"*, and concluding with *"the person"*.
  * **Forensic Interpretation:** Terminology instability indicates an acute psychological shift in perceived relationship, emerging hostility, or an unconscious attempt to depersonalize the counterpart during the commission of a questionable act.

### 2.2 Temporal Lacunae & Chronological Inversions
* **The Rule of Forward Episodic Flow:** Humans recall real events moving forward in time.
* **Inverted Temporal Markers:** Phrases such as *"Before that happened, I had already..."*, *"Prior to leaving, I made sure..."*.
  * An inverted sentence indicates an active **editorial intervention**. The narrator paused forward recall to inject an alibi or cognitive justification before detailing an event that might arouse suspicion.
* **Bridge Phrases (Missing Time):**
  * *"The next thing I knew..."*
  * *"Later on..."*
  * *"Afterwards..."*
  * Every bridge phrase represents an unstated duration where the narrator refuses to commit their actions to verifiable language.

### 2.3 The Social Introduction Protocol
* **Truthful Norm:** Significant participants in an incident are introduced with relational context and appropriate social identifiers (*"My business partner, Alex, arrived at the meeting"*).
* **Deceptive Omission:** Deceivers frequently introduce key actors abruptly, without names, using vague plural or passive phrasing (*"Someone came in"*, *"We agreed"*), concealing the identity or pre-existing intimacy of an accomplice.

### 2.4 Unsolicited Persuasion vs. Factual Transmission
* **Information:** Answers *Who, What, Where, When, and How*.
* **Persuasion / Extraneous Noise:** Answers unasked questions about *Why* (*"I did this because I am always cautious..."*).
* Whenever a statement contains extensive explanations of routine decisions that the investigator did not ask about, the narrator is attempting to manufacture plausibility for an anomalous act.
