# 25. Pragmalinguistics & Speech Act Theory (Austin, Searle & Grice)

## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemological Foundation
Pragmalinguistics investigates how context, implicit assumptions, and communicative intentions generate meaning beyond literal syntax. Founded by **J.L. Austin** (*How to Do Things with Words*, 1962), formalized by **John Searle** (Speech Act Taxonomy, 1969), and mathematically extended by **H. Paul Grice** (The Cooperative Principle and Logic and Conversation, 1975), this pillar provides the forensic apparatus for auditing implicit commitments, communicative sabortage, and performative bad faith.

In corporate boardroom dynamics, settlement negotiations, and executive interrogations, bad actors systematically exploit **pragmatic implicatures** to induce false beliefs in their counterpart without uttering a technically false statement.

---

## 2. John Searle’s Taxonomy of Illocutionary Acts
Every legal and business communication consists of three distinct layers:
1. **Locutionary Act:** The literal acoustic/orthographic utterance.
2. **Illocutionary Force:** The real communicative intention (Promising, Threatening, Stating, Ordering).
3. **Perlocutionary Effect:** The actual psychological effect on the listener (Intimidation, Reassurance, Persuasion).

```
                            SEARLE'S ILLOCUTIONARY CLASSES
┌──────────────────┬───────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│      CLASS       │   DIRECTION OF FIT    │             CORPORATE / LEGAL CONTEXT       │
├──────────────────┼───────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. ASSERTIVES    │ Words-to-World        │ Audit representations, warranties, revenue │
│                  │ (Describe truth)      │ figures, due diligence answers.            │
├──────────────────┼───────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. COMMISSIVES   │ World-to-Words        │ Covenants, guarantees, delivery contracts, │
│                  │ (Commit speaker)      │ indemnity obligations, promissory notes.   │
├──────────────────┼───────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. DIRECTIVES    │ World-to-Words        │ Board resolutions, injunctions, audit      │
│                  │ (Demand action)       │ requests, compliance mandates.             │
├──────────────────┼───────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. DECLARATIONS  │ Double (Words ↔ World)│ Mergers, contract terminations, dividend   │
│                  │ (Change reality)      │ declarations, shareholder authorizations.  │
```

### Felicity Conditions & Performative Bad Faith (Austin):
An illocutionary act is **defective / infelicitous** if it violates:
* **The Sincerity Condition:** A promise executed when the promisor lacks the subjective intention or objective financial capacity to perform.
* **The Essential Condition:** The speaker does not intend the utterance to count as creating an operative obligation.
* **The Preparatory Condition:** The speaker lacks the formal legal jurisdiction or authority to execute the declaration.

## 3. Grice’s Cooperative Principle & Maxim Flouting
Paul Grice proved that efficient human dialogue relies on the **Cooperative Principle**: make your conversational contribution such as is required. When an actor flouts a maxim, they generate a **Conversational Implicature**:

```
                          GRICEAN MAXIMS FORENSIC AUDIT
┌──────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ 1. MAXIM OF QUALITY (Truthfulness)   │ Do not say what you believe to be false│
│                                      │ or that for which you lack evidence.   │
│ • Deceptive Flouting:                │ Half-truths, literal truth misleading. │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 2. MAXIM OF QUANTITY (Informativeness)│ Provide exactly as much info as needed │
│                                      │ (neither more nor less).               │
│ • Deceptive Flouting:                │ Strategic lacunae; over-detailing on   │
│                                      │ trivialities to hide core liabilities. │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 3. MAXIM OF RELATION (Relevance)     │ Be relevant.                           │
│ • Deceptive Flouting:                │ Red-herring deflections, pivoting away │
│                                      │ from audit inquiries.                  │
├──────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 4. MAXIM OF MANNER (Perspicuity)     │ Avoid obscurity, ambiguity, prolixity. │
│ • Deceptive Flouting:                │ Intentional obfuscation, labyrinthine  │
│                                      │ legalese designed to induce fatigue.   │
└──────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Paltering: Deception by Literal Truth (The Bronston Standard)
* In *Bronston v. United States* (1973), a bankrupt businessman was asked whether he had Swiss bank accounts. He replied: *"The company had an account there for about six months."*
* **Forensic Diagnosis:** The statement was literally true, but it violated Grice’s Maxim of Relation and Quantity to implicate: *"I personally never had an account."* (In reality, he had personal accounts holding millions).
* **Subtext Engine Rule:** Every answer that responds to an inquiry regarding $X$ by providing a truthful answer regarding related category $Y$ is flagged as **Paltering / Fraudulent Implicature**.

## 4. Presupposition Triggers & Covert Logic Smuggling
Presuppositions are propositions taken for granted within an utterance. Deceptive communicators use **Presupposition Triggers** to force the counterpart into accepting an unverified fact:

1. **Definite Descriptions:** *"The unauthorized withdrawals by the managing director..."* $\rightarrow$ Presupposes the withdrawals occurred and were unauthorized.
2. **Factive Verbs (know, realize, regret):** *"Management regrets our failure to predict the downturn."* $\rightarrow$ Presupposes that the downturn was objectively unpredictable.
3. **Change-of-State Verbs (stop, continue, resume):** *"Has the subsidiary stopped concealing supplier rebates?"* $\rightarrow$ Presupposes the subsidiary previously concealed supplier rebates.

## 5. Diagnostic Scoring & Quantitative Indices
* **Gricean Deflection Index ($GDI$):**
  $$GDI = \frac{N_{\text{flouted maxims (Quantity + Relation + Manner)}}}{N_{\text{direct inquiries}}}$$
  * $GDI > 0.30$ in an executive deposition or audit interview indicates coordinated evasion.
* **Paltering Coefficient ($PC$):**
  $$PC = \frac{N_{\text{literal truth misleading answers}}}{N_{\text{total critical inquiries}}}$$
  * A non-zero $PC$ triggers mandatory forensic cross-examination under the SUE protocol.
