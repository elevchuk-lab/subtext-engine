## 1. Executive Summary: The Western Baseline Bias

The vast majority of Western forensic deception models (and AI training corpora) suffer from **Western, Educated, Industrialized, Rich, Democratic (WEIRD) bias**:
- They assume that a truthful person speaks with linear chronological directness, looks directly at authority, and presents a dry, unadorned sequence of facts.
- Alicia Nortje and international researchers demonstrated that applying these monocultural assumptions to multi-ethnic populations (in Africa, Asia, and Latin America) generates **catastrophic false-positive rates**, misinterpreting cultural communication norms as deception.

> **"What Western investigators view as 'evasion' or 'vagueness' is frequently the respectful, high-context linguistic norm of a traditional culture. Forensic accuracy requires calibrating the baseline against the speaker's cultural discourse tradition before flagging deception."**

### 1. Narrative Circularity vs. Intentional Evasion
* In traditional African and Latin American discourse, beginning a narrative with community context, ancestral background, or metaphorical framing is an expression of thoroughness and honor.
* **Calibration Rule:** If an author uses circular narrative framing throughout their entire discourse (including when discussing neutral topics), it is a **cultural baseline**, not an evasion technique.

### 2. The Collective Pronoun Baseline
* In collectivist cultures, individuals routinely use *"we"*, *"our team"*, or *"the elders"* even when recounting personal actions.
* Unlike Western deception (where switching from *"I"* to *"we"* indicates guilt dilution), in collectivist cultures it reflects communal identity. Subtext Engine checks for sudden *changes* in pronoun style rather than penalizing collective pronouns outright.
