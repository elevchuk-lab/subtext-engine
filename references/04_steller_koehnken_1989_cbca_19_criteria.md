# Criteria-Based Content Analysis (CBCA): The 19 Foundational Criteria

**Architects:** Max Steller, Ph.D. & Günter Köhnken, Ph.D.  
**Seminal Publication:** *"Criteria-Based Content Analysis"* (1989)  
**Parent Framework:** Statement Validity Assessment (SVA)  
**Historical Origin:** Undeutsch Hypothesis (Udo Undeutsch, 1954, 1967, 1982)  
**Primary Field:** Forensic Psychology / Evidentiary Credibility Assessment in Judicial Systems  

---

## 1. Executive Summary & The Undeutsch Hypothesis

Criteria-Based Content Analysis (CBCA) is the central diagnostic component of Statement Validity Assessment (SVA), a comprehensive forensic methodology accepted across European Supreme Courts (notably Germany, Sweden, the Netherlands, and Spain) for evaluating the credibility of witness and victim statements.

CBCA is grounded upon the **Undeutsch Hypothesis**, formulated by German psychologist Udo Undeutsch:
> **"A statement derived from the memory of actual, self-experienced events differs qualitatively and structurally from a statement that is the product of imagination, fabrication, coaching, or autosuggestion."**

### Core Axiom:
Truthful statements contain specific, idiosyncratic cognitive and linguistic qualities that a deceiver or fabulist cannot or will not incorporate into an invented story. Liars typically lack the cognitive capacity to invent plausible, fine-grained, self-incriminating details while simultaneously managing the cognitive load of appearing honest.

> **CRITICAL FORENSIC RULE:**  
> The **presence** of CBCA criteria indicates veracity and real experiential memory.  
> However, the **absence** of these criteria does *not* automatically prove deception (it may indicate limited verbal capacity, trauma-induced amnesia, developmental age, or poor interview technique).

## 2. The 19 Criteria Taxonomy

CBCA divides its 19 criteria into four distinct thematic categories:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE 19 CBCA CRITERIA                 │
├─────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┤
│ CATEGORY I              │ General Characteristics      │
│ (Global Structure)      │ Criteria 1 - 3               │
├─────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
│ CATEGORY II             │ Specific Contents            │
│ (Experiential Recall)   │ Criteria 4 - 13              │
├─────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
│ CATEGORY III            │ Motivation-Related Contents  │
│ (Psychological Posture) │ Criteria 14 - 18             │
├─────────────────────────┼──────────────────────────────┤
│ CATEGORY IV             │ Offense-Specific Elements    │
│ (Criminological Fit)    │ Criterion 19                 │
└─────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┘
```

### Category I: General Characteristics (Global Structural Integrity)

#### 1. Logical Consistency
* **Definition:** The components of the statement cohere logically, without internal contradictions, physical impossibilities, or cognitive non-sequiturs.
* **Forensic Significance:** Fabricated stories often crack under detailed cross-examination when secondary claims conflict with primary premises.

#### 2. Unstructured Production
* **Definition:** The testimony is presented in an unpolished, fragmented, or non-chronological order rather than a smooth, rehearsed, three-act chronological storyline.
* **Forensic Significance:** Natural human episodic memory is associative and non-linear. A narrator jumps from an emotion to an observation, then remembers an earlier detail. Fabricators rehearse their stories chronologically (*"A happened, then B happened, then C happened"*) and struggle to narrate out of sequence.

#### 3. Quantity of Details
* **Definition:** The narrative contains an abundance of specific, concrete details regarding individuals, objects, movements, locations, and timeframes.
* **Forensic Significance:** Honest recall generates high detail density. Deceptive subjects provide broad abstractions to minimize the risk of being pinned down on verifiable facts.

---

### Category II: Specific Contents (Experiential Sensory Anchoring)

#### 4. Contextual Embedding
* **Definition:** The critical event is firmly embedded into the ongoing spatial, temporal, and social fabric of the narrator's everyday life (e.g., routines, background obligations, family dynamics).
* **Forensic Significance:** Fabricated accounts appear isolated in an artificial vacuum, stripped of the routine background complexities of real life.

#### 5. Descriptions of Interactions
* **Definition:** Accounts of mutual, dynamic reactions between participants (e.g., verbal exchanges, mutual physical adjustments, shifts in body language, reciprocal cause-and-effect).
* **Forensic Significance:** Deceptive accounts usually describe unilateral action without capturing dynamic interpersonal push-and-pull.

#### 6. Reproduction of Conversation
* **Definition:** Verbatim or direct quoting of dialogue, including idiosyncratic speech patterns, slurs, peculiar phrasing, or specialized terminology.
* **Forensic Significance:** Quoting direct speech requires episodic auditory recall. Fabricated narratives summarize conversations generically (*"We talked about the project and agreed"*).

#### 7. Unexpected Complications / Disruptions
* **Definition:** Mention of unforeseen disruptions, equipment malfunctions, external interruptions, or obstacles that interfered with the expected course of events.
* **Forensic Significance:** Liars construct clean, streamlined scenarios. They rarely introduce random complications that disrupt their narrative arc.

#### 8. Unusual Details
* **Definition:** Inclusion of bizarre, surprising, or idiosyncratic details that violate typical stereotypes or scripts of the event.
* **Forensic Significance:** If an event is invented, the author relies on generic archetypes. True episodic experience is messy, eccentric, and counter-intuitive.

#### 9. Superfluous / Peripheral Details
* **Definition:** The inclusion of minor, irrelevant peripheral details that do not advance the plot or contribute to proving the narrator's case.
* **Forensic Significance:** Deceptive authors discard non-functional elements. Honest narrators naturally recall incidental elements surrounding trauma or conflict.

#### 10. Accurately Reported Details Misunderstood
* **Definition:** The narrator accurately describes an action, object, or term, but clearly misunderstood its objective meaning or significance at the time.
* **Forensic Significance:** Virtually impossible to fabricate. A witness cannot invent a phenomenon that they do not understand.

#### 11. Related Associations
* **Definition:** Spontaneous references to thoughts, external memories, or parallel obligations triggered during the event (e.g., *"While he was shouting, I noticed the clock and remembered I hadn't fed the cat"*).
* **Forensic Significance:** Reflects genuine cognitive multi-tasking during real-world experience.

#### 12. Subjective Mental State of the Witness
* **Definition:** Explicit description of internal feelings, physiological reactions, or cognitive processes experienced during the incident (e.g., terror, disgust, dizziness, shock).
* **Forensic Significance:** Demonstrates experiential cognitive retrieval rather than intellectual storytelling.

#### 13. Attributed Mental State of the Perpetrator / Other
* **Definition:** Observations and interpretations regarding the emotions, motivations, or intentions of the counterpart (*"He seemed terrified of what he had just done"*).
* **Forensic Significance:** Honest observers naturally attempt to decode the internal states of their counterparts during intense encounters.

---

### Category III: Motivation-Related Contents (Psychological Posture)

#### 14. Spontaneous Corrections
* **Definition:** The narrator corrects themselves spontaneously without external prompting (*"He was wearing a blue jacket—wait, no, it was a dark green windbreaker"*).
* **Forensic Significance:** Liars perceive self-correction as a sign of weakness or vulnerability that damages credibility; genuine witnesses correct themselves in real time as memory clarifies.

#### 15. Admitting Lack of Memory
* **Definition:** Freely conceding memory failures (*"I don't remember what he said after that"*, *"My mind went completely blank"*).
* **Forensic Significance:** Liars feel compelled to know everything to appear credible. Honest witnesses readily admit the biological limitations of memory.

#### 16. Raising Doubts About One's Own Testimony
* **Definition:** Expressing skepticism or self-criticism regarding elements of their own story (*"I know this sounds completely absurd, but..."*).
* **Forensic Significance:** Fabricators strive for unassailable plausibility; genuine witnesses are comfortable expressing disbelief at their own strange reality.

#### 17. Self-Deprecation
* **Definition:** Mentioning personal errors, embarrassing behaviors, cowardice, or poor judgment that cast the narrator in an unfavorable light.
* **Forensic Significance:** Deceptive storytellers strive to project heroism, victimhood, or competence. Voluntarily disclosing embarrassing personal flaws correlates heavily with truth.

#### 18. Pardoning the Perpetrator / Opponent
* **Definition:** Showing reluctance to condemn the opponent, offering excuses for their behavior, or minimizing their culpability (*"I don't think he meant to hurt me, he was under immense stress"*).
* **Forensic Significance:** Fabricated allegations are typically motivated by malice, revenge, or legal leverage, leading the fabricator to maximize the counterpart's perceived villainy.

---

### Category IV: Offense-Specific Elements

#### 19. Details Characteristic of the Specific Offense / Violation
* **Definition:** The presence of specialized operational patterns, psychological escalation cycles, or subtle dynamics known to criminologists/auditors but obscure to laypersons.
* **Forensic Significance:** Indicates that the narrative aligns with empirical criminological reality rather than cinematic cliches.

## 3. CBCA Scoring Protocol for Subtext Engine

In automated text auditing, each criterion is evaluated on a tri-state qualitative scale:
- **0 = Absent:** No textual evidence of criterion.
- **1 = Partially Present:** Weak or ambiguous manifestations.
- **2 = Strongly Present:** Unambiguous, vivid, and detailed realization.

$$\text{CBCA Credibility Index} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{19} C_i}{38} \times 100\%$$

* **High Veracity Threshold (> 60%):** Strong evidence of experiential episodic memory.
* **Ambiguous / Vulnerable (30% - 60%):** Requires targeted corroboration.
* **Low / Fabricated Profile (< 30%):** Absence of cognitive complexity; high risk of rehearsal, coaching, or fabrication.
